**Re:** CSAT slide, the bottom ten, and where the Q3 training budget should go

**The problem.** CSAT averaged 3.50 from January to November 2025 and fell to 2.95 in February 2026. You asked who to retrain, with Rs 4 lakh available.

**Key finding: most of the slide is a product problem, not a people problem.** Pulse 2 earbuds from three manufacturing lots (made October to December 2025, lot codes PL2-2510, 2511 and 2512) are linked to a replacement on 42% of orders. Every other Pulse 2 lot is at 6.5%. Customers on those tickets rate us 2.72 on average, against 3.46 on all other tickets. Without them, February CSAT is 3.32 rather than 2.95. Replacements are concentrated in those lots, so festive volume alone does not explain the rise.

**The number.** About 709 replacements were avoidable, at Rs 1,820 each (unit cost Rs 1,480 plus Rs 340 logistics, per the support policy; Finance's Rs 2,500 is not supported by it). That is about Rs 12.9 lakh over the life of those lots, and about 515 replacements, or Rs 9.4 lakh, in Q1 2026 alone. Goal: bring lot-linked excess replacements from about 515 a quarter to roughly zero, worth about Rs 9.4 lakh a quarter. These are lower bounds, because 8% of tickets cannot be traced to a lot. Fixing this sits with the supplier and quality teams; support's part is flagging a bad lot early.

**Who to review.** The raw bottom ten is six Tier 2 warranty agents and the four-person chat hardware triage rota. They handle the defective-lot and warranty queues, so their raw scores mostly measure the queue. After comparing like with like (same issue type, channel, product, defective lot, and team), five people still sit clearly below peers, each with 89 to 142 survey responses: Siddharth Kapoor, Siddharth Trivedi, Kavya Pandey (the chat agent, A3006, not the Logistics namesake), Zaid Khanna, and Jaspreet Desai. This shows a performance signal, not cause: we cannot see how hard each conversation really was, and the triage rota may still get tougher cases than the data shows.
